# Aerobic Base Progression and Nutritional Guidelines (Maffetone Method)

This guide covers long-term aerobic base building, managing cardiac drift, and nutritional support for fat adaptation.

---

## 1. Managing Cardiac Drift

During extended runs in warm conditions or under dehydration:
- **Cardiac Drift** occurs: Heart rate gradually rises even if pace remains constant.
- **MAF Enforcement**: When cardiac drift occurs, the runner **must slow down** to keep heart rate below the MAF ceiling. If pace slows to a brisk walk, the runner walks.
- Over weeks of training, stroke volume increases, hydration regulation improves, and cardiac drift diminishes.

---

## 2. When to Introduce Anaerobic Work

Maffetone strongly advises against adding speed or tempo workouts until the athlete has established a true aerobic base:
1. **Criteria to Add Speed**:
   - The runner has trained for at least 3–4 months exclusively at MAF.
   - Monthly MAF tests have plateaued (i.e. no further pace improvement for two consecutive tests at MAF HR).
   - Zero signs of musculoskeletal pain or systemic fatigue.
2. **Speed Work Limitations**:
   - Limit speed/anaerobic sessions to a 4 to 6-week window before a target race.
   - Speed sessions should comprise no more than 10–15% of total weekly volume.

---

## 3. Nutritional Support for Fat Burning

- Reduce refined carbohydrates and added sugars outside of training to encourage cellular fat oxidation.
- During MAF runs under 90 minutes, train fasted or with water/electrolytes only.
- For long runs over 2 hours, ingest balanced electrolytes and moderate carbohydrates as needed to support recovery.
