# MAF 180 Rules, Calculations, and Test Protocol

This reference details the physiological rules and testing standards of Dr. Phil Maffetone's Maximum Aerobic Function system.

---

## 1. Physiological Rationale

At heart rates below the MAF ceiling:
- The body burns predominantly **free fatty acids** rather than limited glycogen stores.
- Sympathetic nervous system arousal (adrenaline, cortisol) is minimal.
- Lactic acid accumulation is virtually zero ($< 1.5\text{ mmol/L}$).
- Autonomic recovery is rapid, allowing higher weekly mileage without systemic fatigue.

---

## 2. Standardized MAF Test Protocol

Runners perform the MAF Test every 3 to 4 weeks under identical conditions:

1. **Conditions**:
   - Standard 400-meter track (avoids traffic, stoplights, and elevation variables).
   - Calm, cool weather (or climate-controlled treadmill at 1.0% grade).
   - Accurate chest-strap heart rate monitor (optical wrist sensors can suffer from cadence lock).
2. **Warm-up**:
   - 15 minutes of slow jogging, starting at $\text{MAF}-20\text{ bpm}$ and gradually rising to $\text{MAF}-5\text{ bpm}$.
3. **Execution**:
   - Run 5 continuous miles (or 8 km).
   - Lock heart rate onto exact MAF (e.g., if MAF is 145 bpm, stay between 143 and 145 bpm).
   - Lap each mile/km split.
4. **Sample Evaluation**:

*Progression Note*: Mile splits naturally slow down over the 5 miles due to cardiac drift; as fitness improves, both the overall pace and cardiac drift decline.
