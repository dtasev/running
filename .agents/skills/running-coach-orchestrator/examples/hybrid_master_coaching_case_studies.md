# Hybrid Coaching Case Studies & Plan Synthesis

This document illustrates how an expert coach combines elements from multiple training systems to address real-world athlete challenges.

---

## Case Study 1: The Overtrained Runner with Plateaued 10k/HM Times
- **Profile**: 38-year-old female, 5k PR 21:30, HM PR 1:42:00. Feeling constantly exhausted, frequent calf strains.
- **Diagnosis**: Chronically running Easy runs too fast (in the "grey zone" between aerobic and threshold), elevated resting HR and cortisol.
- **Hybrid Solution**:
  1. **Weeks 1–8 (Maffetone Base Reset)**: 100% of running capped at MAF HR ($180 - 38 = 142\text{ bpm}$). Weekly volume: 35–40 mpw.
  2. **Weeks 9–16 (Daniels 2Q Half Marathon Build)**: Introduce two quality days per week at exact VDOT paces (Threshold Cruise Intervals: $4 \times 1.5\text{ mi @ T}$), keeping all non-Q days strictly at MAF HR.
- **Result**: PR of 1:36:45 at target Half Marathon with zero injury flare-ups.

## Case Study 2: The Busy Executive Targeting a Boston Qualifier
- **Profile**: 44-year-old male, current marathon PR 3:28:00, seeking a sub-3:10:00 (BQ). Can only run 4 days during the week due to work travel.
- **Diagnosis**: Needs higher lactate threshold velocity and marathon-specific endurance, but cannot commit to Hansons' 6-day cumulative fatigue schedule or Pfitzinger's daily volume.
- **Hybrid Solution**:
  1. **Jack Daniels 2Q Architecture**: Tuesday Midweek Quality (T-pace Cruise intervals or VO2max reps) + Sunday Long Run with MP blocks ($18\text{ mi}$ with $12\text{ mi @ MP}$).
  2. **Thursday Midweek MLR**: 11–13 miles at Pfitz Endurance Zone (75–80% HR).
  3. **Saturday Easy Run**: 5–6 miles easy + strides.
- **Result**: Ran 3:08:12 at the Chicago Marathon, achieving a comfortable BQ.

## Case Study 3: The Injury-Prone Master Runner Tackling First Marathon
- **Profile**: 52-year-old male, history of knee meniscus irritation and plantar fasciitis. Wants to complete the New York City Marathon safely.
- **Hybrid Solution**:
  1. **Jeff Galloway Run-Walk-Run Framework**: Run 30s / Walk 30s from mile 1 of every training run.
  2. **Every-Other-Weekend Long Run**: Long runs scheduled every 14 days, building progressively to 26 miles at 12:30 min/mile.
  3. **Cross-Training Days**: 2 days/week of cycling and low-impact strength training.
- **Result**: Finished the NYC Marathon in 4:48:30 smiling, injury-free, with zero walking in the final 6 miles.
