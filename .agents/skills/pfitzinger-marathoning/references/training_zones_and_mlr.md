# Pfitzinger Training Zones and Medium-Long Run (MLR) Guide

This reference details the physiological mechanisms behind Pete Pfitzinger's training zones and the critical execution rules for Medium-Long Runs (MLRs).

---

## 1. Physiological Training Zones

### Recovery Run
- **Heart Rate**: $< 76\%$ Max HR ($< 70\%$ HRR)
- **Duration**: 30 to 50 minutes (typically 4–6 miles / 6–10 km)
- **Physiology**: Promotes blood flow to tired muscles, helps clear metabolic byproducts, and aids soft tissue healing. Must be kept truly easy; going too fast impairs the next quality day.

### General Aerobic (GA)
- **Heart Rate**: $70\% - 81\%$ Max HR ($62\% - 75\%$ HRR)
- **Duration**: 45 to 70 minutes (typically 6–10 miles / 10–16 km)
- **Physiology**: Enhances capillarization, increases mitochondrial density, and conditions musculoskeletal structures without excessive recovery time.
- **Strides**: Often appended with $6-8 \times 100\text{m}$ strides (accentuating quick turnover, upright posture, and arm drive).

### Endurance / Long Run / Medium-Long Run (MLR)
- **Heart Rate**: $73\% - 84\%$ Max HR ($65\% - 78\%$ HRR)
- **Duration**: 75 to 150 minutes (typically 11–22 miles / 18–35 km)
- **Pacing**: Progressive. Start approximately 20% slower than marathon pace, then settle into 15% slower, and finish the final 3–5 miles around 10% slower than marathon pace.
- **Physiology**: Glycogen depletion adaptation, upgrading slow-twitch muscle fibers, fat oxidation optimization.

### Marathon Pace (MP)
- **Heart Rate**: $82\% - 88\%$ Max HR ($73\% - 84\%$ HRR)
- **Duration**: 8 to 14 miles embedded within a 14–20 mile long run.
- **Physiology**: Specific race rhythm, psychological readiness, practicing race-day in-run fueling (gels, electrolytes) at high speed.

### Lactate Threshold (LT)
- **Heart Rate**: $85\% - 91\%$ Max HR ($77\% - 88\%$ HRR)
- **Pace**: Current 15k to Half Marathon race pace (for most runners, the pace they could sustain for 50–60 minutes in an all-out effort).
- **Structure**: Continuous tempo runs of 4–7 miles (6–11 km) or structured cruise tempo intervals (e.g., $2 \times 3\text{ miles @ LT}$ with 3 min rest).
- **Physiology**: Improves lactate clearance rate and pushes the lactate curve to the right.

### VO2max Intervals
- **Heart Rate**: $93\% - 98\%$ Max HR ($90\% - 95\%$ HRR)
- **Pace**: 3k to 5k race pace.
- **Structure**: Intervals of 600m to 1600m with 50% to 90% jog recovery time (e.g., $5 \times 1000\text{m @ 5k pace}$ with 2:30 min jog).
- **Physiology**: Increases maximal oxygen consumption, stroke volume, and cardiac output.

## 2. The Midweek Medium-Long Run (MLR) Rulebook

1. **Why MLRs Are Essential**:
   In traditional marathon training, runners only run long once per week (Sunday). Pfitzinger proved that adding a second substantial run mid-week (Wednesday) accelerates endurance adaptations and mirrors the back-half of a marathon without requiring weekly 20+ milers.
2. **Standard Distance Targets**:
   - 55 MPW Plan: 11 to 12 miles (18–19 km)
   - 70 MPW Plan: 12 to 15 miles (19–24 km)
   - 85 MPW Plan: Two MLRs (e.g., 11 mi on Tuesday, 14 mi on Thursday)
3. **Execution Mistakes to Avoid**:
   - *Racing the MLR*: Turning it into an unofficial tempo run leads to overtraining by the weekend.
   - *Under-fueling*: Treat the MLR like a serious session; hydrate and take a light carbohydrate snack before the run.
